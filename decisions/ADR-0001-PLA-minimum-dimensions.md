# ADR-0001: Primary Landing Area minimum dimensions for new DZ certification

## Status

**Proposed** — for WG review

## Date

2026-05-09 _(initial proposal date; final decision date pending WG vote)_

## Decision

The WG proposes that the minimum Primary Landing Area (PLA) for new DZ certification be **300 m × 300 m at Tier 1**, **250 m × 250 m at Tier 2**, and **200 m × 200 m at Tier 3**, all measured as clear and level area, with surface and obstacle-clearance criteria as specified in §2A.4.1 and §2A.4.3 of the Framework.

## Context

Establishing a minimum PLA dimension is the single most consequential physical specification for a new dropzone. Set it too high and Indian land-availability constraints prevent any new DZs from coming online outside metropolitan corridors. Set it too low and student-canopy outlanding incidents and pattern-conflict near-misses become normalised.

The Framework's Tier 1 figure of 300 m × 300 m is at the lower end of global benchmarks. USPA's typical Group Member expectations for student-progression DZs are closer to 330 m on the radius interpretation. APF site approvals frequently exceed these figures for student operations. The contested point is whether India should align with these international benchmarks (legitimacy advantage; safer student outcomes) or accept tighter minima (more sites possible; aligns with realistic Indian land availability).

This ADR addresses §2A.4.1 of the Framework.

## Options considered

### Option 1 — Align with global benchmarks (≥ 330 m × 330 m at Tier 1)

- **Description:** Set Tier 1 minimum at 330 m × 330 m, matching USPA Group Member typical expectations.
- **Pros:** Strongest student-safety margin; clean alignment with international peer federations; less defensive justification needed when seeking reciprocity.
- **Cons:** Materially fewer candidate sites in India satisfy this without significant land aggregation. Risks pushing prospective operators into informal/unregulated operation outside the Framework. Tier 2 and Tier 3 minima would also need to scale up proportionally, further restricting access.
- **Sources:** USPA SIM 5-2.E (student program areas); APF Operational Regulations (site approval guidance).

### Option 2 — India-tiered minima (300 / 250 / 200) — _proposed_

- **Description:** As specified in the current §2A.4.1.
- **Pros:** Realistic against Indian land availability; tier structure scales required infrastructure to operational tempo and student exposure; below-minimum sites are clearly excluded; obstacle-clearance and surface criteria compensate for tighter dimensions.
- **Cons:** Tier 1 minimum is below typical USPA Group Member expectations; will require defensive explanation in any future reciprocity discussions; provides less margin for student outlanding events.
- **Sources:** Framework §2A.4.1; field assessment of currently-active and prospective Indian DZ sites.

### Option 3 — Performance-based criteria (no fixed dimension)

- **Description:** Replace fixed dimensions with a performance test — e.g., "PLA must be sized such that a student under standard canopy at the published wing-loading can reach the PLA from any normal pattern entry, with documented margin."
- **Pros:** Most rigorous safety basis; site-specific. Theoretically matches site to actual operational profile.
- **Cons:** Requires expertise to assess; opens room for litigated interpretation; harder to inspect; harder for prospective operators to plan against. Likely to default to international benchmarks anyway in practice.
- **Sources:** General performance-based regulation literature; ICAO PBN principles (analogous logic).

## Decision rationale

_To be completed after WG vote._

The proposed direction is Option 2 because:

1. It produces an inspectable, plannable standard that prospective operators can build against.
2. It is honest about the tradeoff — the Framework explicitly notes that the Tier 1 figure is at the lower end of global benchmarks.
3. The compensating obstacle-clearance, surface, and outs criteria (§2A.4.2 and §2A.4.3) materially close the safety gap.
4. Tier 2/3 reductions reflect lower operational tempo and lower student exposure, not lower safety standards.
5. The provisional certification period (§2A.10.5) provides operational evidence before specialty endorsements (wingsuit, AFF) are granted, partially compensating for tighter dimensions during the riskiest learning phase.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Realistic pathway for new Indian DZs outside metro corridors.
- Clear, inspectable standard.
- Tier scaling allows DZ ecosystem growth at sustainable pace.

### Negative

- Tier 1 PLA below typical USPA Group Member expectation; will require explanation in reciprocity discussions.
- Less margin for student canopy outlandings under unusual wind events.
- May need revisiting if incident data over Years 1–3 shows clustering around outlandings on PLA boundary.

### Implementation impact

- §2A.4.1 — current text reflects this decision.
- §2A.4.3 (obstacle clearance) — must be enforced strictly to compensate.
- Part 8 audit checklist — line item must verify PLA dimensions at certification and at every annual audit.
- Year-3 review — incident data should be re-examined with PLA dimension correlation; this ADR may be superseded.
